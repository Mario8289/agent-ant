Interview script

Mom Test · 30–40 min · v0.4 (2026-11-17)
Interview #
Date
Trade

Their life, not your idea. Past specifics, not opinions. Talk ~20% of the time.

  • Never mention the app — not even at the end. Never mention a price.
  • Wait 3 seconds after each answer. Chase specifics: “When was the last time? What happened?”
  • If they complain: “tell me more”, never “I could fix that”. Quiet room, no background talk.
  • Don't lead with a task. Their top 3 sets the section order and where the time goes.
  • Never ask: “Is this a good idea?” · “Would you use an app that…?” · “How much would you pay?” · “What would your dream app do?” · “Don't you find it annoying when…?”
“I'm trying to get my head round how people in the trade actually run the business side of things — everything that isn't the work itself. Can I ask you a few things about how it really works for you? No agenda, I'm just curious.”

0 · Who they are segment

  1. Roughly how much of your work comes from private customers, and how much through a company or main contractor?
  2. Where did your last three jobs come from?
  3. How long have you been trading on your own? Anyone helping with the books?

1 · The admin, unprompted H1, H9, H10 — ask before anything specific

  1. What's the most boring or frustrating part of the business side for you?
  2. What else? (get three, in their words)
  3. When did you last spend time on that? What were you doing?
    • 6a. Think about last week. Roughly how many hours went on the business side, everything that wasn't the work itself? (a number for that week, not “usually”)
    • 6b. When did those hours happen: in the working day, evenings, weekends? (H10 counts evenings and weekends only; get the split)

Then set the order: sections matching their top 3 first, most time there. Cover the rest briefly but ask every question.

A · Phone calls H1, G0b

  1. How do calls from customers reach you during the day, and what happens to them?
  2. When was the last time you missed a call from a customer? What happened next?
  3. What did you do — ring back, text, nothing?
  4. Did that one turn into a job, or did you lose it?
  5. How often in a normal week? (then: what happened last week specifically?)
  6. When you lose a job that way, what does it cost you, roughly?
  7. Last time you rang a tradesperson and got a text back instead of an answer, what did you do?

Listen for: a real call, a real lost job, a number. “I don't ring them back” → ask why, and whether that work matters.

B · Enquiries and quotes

  1. Talk me through a new customer from first contact to starting the job.
  2. How do you do quotes now — pen and paper, phone, computer?
  3. Where do you get material prices when pricing a job?
  4. How long did your last quote take, start to finish? When did you do it?

C · Invoices and getting paid H4

  1. How do you do invoices — who, how, when?
  2. When was the last time someone was late paying? What did you do?
  3. How much are you owed right now?

D · Receipts, job costs and year-end H9

  1. Where do the receipts for a job go while it's running?
  2. Walk me through the last job you totalled up. How long did it take?
  3. When did you last lose a receipt? What happened?
  4. Supplier invoices that come by email — what happens to them?
  5. Walk me through getting your year-end paperwork ready. How long did it take last year?
  6. Who does your accounts? What do you hand them, and what do you still do yourself?
  7. What does that cost you?

Listen for: hours, lost receipts, evenings and weekends. Ask to see a real job's paperwork.

E · What they have already tried

  1. Have you ever tried any software or an app for any of this?
  2. What happened — stuck with it or binned it? Why?
  3. What do you use your phone for, business-wise, day to day?
  4. Who else in the trade has this sorted? What do they do?

F · Commitment ask H3, H7 — pick one, tick what they actually did

CurrencyAskGiven?
Time“Could I sit with you next time you [their top pain] and watch how you do it?”
Reputation“Know one or two others in the trade I could have the same chat with? Could you intro me?”
CashOnly once something exists: a deposit or pre-order. Never a hypothetical price.

“Sounds good, let me know” = politeness. A booked time, a made intro or a deposit = signal.
