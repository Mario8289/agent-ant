# Recruiting Interviewees 2–6

## Purpose

Find five traders who **mostly do private work** and get a 30–40 minute conversation with
each, using [interview script](./interview-script.md) v0.4. This is issue
[#140](https://github.com/Mario8289/agent-ant/issues/140), and the G0a decision (#143) waits
on it.

Interview 1 showed why the screen matters. Dad mostly subcontracts, so missed private
enquiries cost him little, and his answers say more about his segment than about the problem.
Another subcontract trader would use up one of the five slots.

## Who counts

Ask the screening question before booking. Book only people who pass all three.

| Check | Ask | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| Private work | "Roughly how much of your work is for homeowners directly, and how much comes through a company or main contractor?" | **More than half** direct to homeowners |
| Runs their own books | "Is it just you, or do you have someone doing the paperwork?" | Sole trader or 1–2 people. Nobody employed to do the admin |
| Has done a year-end | "How long have you been on your own?" | At least a year, so they have been through a tax return |

**Spread the five.** Use at least three different trades (for example plumber, electrician,
decorator, plasterer, landscaper, roofer) and no more than one more carpenter. Recruit from at
least three of the channels below. Five people from one Facebook group, or five from one
trade, would make the result say more about where they were found than about traders.

**At most one warm contact.** Friends of the family want to be encouraging, the same bias
interview 1 had. Strangers, or people one step removed, give cleaner answers.

## Where to find them

In rough order of how well each works for private-work traders:

| Channel | Why it finds private-work traders | How |
|---|---|---|
| **People who recently hired someone** | A homeowner who hired a trader directly found a private-work trader | Ask friends and colleagues: "Anyone had a tradesperson in recently who was good?" Ask them to pass on the [intro message](#2-intro-via-a-customer). This is the warmest route that is still a stranger |
| **Local community Facebook groups** | "Can anyone recommend a plumber?" threads list traders who live on private recommendations | Note the names recommended, then contact them through their public business page or number |
| **Lead-gen listings** (Checkatrade, MyBuilder, Rated People, Nextdoor) | Traders paying for leads depend on private work | Contact through the business phone or website on the listing. **Never** post a fake job or send a fake enquiry to reach them |
| **Trade counters early morning** (builders' merchants, Screwfix, Toolstation), roughly 6:30–8:00 | Busy, but traders are there and it's face to face | Don't approach anyone in the queue. Catch them outside, use the [one-liner](#3-in-person), and offer to call later. Ask the branch before leaving a notice |
| **Trade groups online** (trade Facebook groups, r/uktrades) | Self-selected, so it skews to people who like talking shop | Post the [group message](#4-group-post) only where the group's rules allow it |
| **Dad's network** | Few of his contacts will pass the screen if most are subcontract too | Ask him specifically for traders who "do mostly their own customers" |

## Messages

None of these mention an app, a product or a price. They are honest: you're researching how
the business side works, and that is true.

### 1. Direct text or message

> Hi, I got your number from [where]. I'm looking into how tradespeople handle the business
> side — the paperwork, quotes, calls, all the non-trade stuff. Not selling anything. Could I
> buy you a coffee for 30 minutes and ask how it works for you? Happy to fit round your jobs.

### 2. Intro via a customer

For a friend to forward to a trader they hired:

> My friend [name] is looking into how tradespeople handle the business side of things and
> asked if I knew anyone good. Would you be up for a 30-minute chat over a coffee? Nothing to
> sell. Can I pass on your number?

### 3. In person

> Sorry to grab you — I'm looking into how tradespeople deal with the paperwork side of the
> job. Not selling anything. Could I buy you a coffee one day this week and ask you about it?

### 4. Group post

> Looking for 5 sole traders who mostly work for homeowners (any trade) for a 30-minute chat
> about how you handle the business side: quotes, invoices, receipts, calls, year-end. I'm not
> selling anything. Coffee or a bacon roll on me, at a time that suits you. Message me if
> you're up for it.

### If they ask "what's it for?"

Tell the truth without pitching: "I'm trying to work out whether there's a better way to do
some of this. I don't have anything to show you, and I'd rather hear how you do it now." Then
move on. If they push for details, say you'll happily tell them at the end. At the end, don't
tell them; thank them, and ask about the intro (Section F).

## Practicalities

- **Numbers.** Expect about one yes for every three or four asks. Plan on 15–20 asks for five
  interviews, spread over two to three weeks.
- **In person if you can.** Section D asks to *see* a real job's paperwork. A kitchen table
  beats a phone call. A call is fine if it's the only way to get the interview.
- **Thank them small.** A coffee, a bacon roll, or a £10 voucher. Anything bigger starts to
  buy goodwill, and goodwill turns into polite answers.
- **Book interview 2 before contacting everyone.** Running one interview first shows whether
  v0.4 works in practice. Fix anything that doesn't before interview 3, and record the change
  in the script's change history.
- **Ask for intros at the end** (Section F). Someone who gives an intro has shown real
  commitment (H7), and that intro is your next interviewee.

## Keeping it out of the repo

- Keep the contact list (names, numbers, where you found them, status) **outside git**: a
  spreadsheet on your machine or a notes app. Nothing that identifies a person goes into
  `docs/`, issues or PRs (AGENTS.md rule 5).
- In the [findings](./findings.md) roster, use a label such as "2 — Plumber", not a name.
- Progress on #140 can be counts only: "asked 12, 4 yes, 2 booked, 1 done".
- **This is research, not marketing.** Contacting a sole trader for a genuine research
  conversation is different from sending them promotional messages, which UK rules (PECR)
  restrict for sole traders. Keep it that way: no product mention, no offers, no follow-up
  marketing to anyone you contacted. This is a reading of the rules, not legal advice.

## Anti-patterns

- ⚠️ **Recruiting only people you know.** Fast, but the answers come back polite.
- ⚠️ **Skipping the screen** because someone's keen. A keen subcontract trader is still the
  wrong segment.
- ⚠️ **Pitching to get a yes.** "I'm building an app that…" gets more yeses and worse
  interviews. Every answer after a pitch is unusable (interview 1).
- ⚠️ **Fake enquiries.** Posing as a customer to get a trader on the phone is dishonest and
  wastes their time. Ask for the chat directly.
