## Decision checkpoint

- **≥3 of 6** confirm missed calls as top-3 pain **with a real past example and a lost job**
  → proceed to the build.
- **<3 of 6** → do not build. Re-segment or change the wedge. Per Ries this is validated
  learning, not failure.

## Interview notes

### 1 — Dad (carpenter)

**G0a: does not count.** He misses calls daily but recalled no specific call and no lost job,
and says he lets private enquiries go because that work is extra to his subcontract income.

**Evidence quality.** About 45 minutes, transcribed in Descript; recording and transcript are
kept out of the repo. Background conversation in the room was excluded, and speaker labels
were corrected by context. From about 43:54 the interviewer described the product and
suggested £20 and £50 a month; the second half drifted into feature discussion. Positive
reactions after that point are not evidence.

**Segment.** He is not the target segment. Most of his income is subcontract work, so a
missed private enquiry costs him little. His result says more about segment than about the
wedge: interviews 2–6 should be traders who depend on private work (Section 0 of the script).

**Strongest signal.** Unprompted, he named year-end accounts and getting paperwork up to date
as the most frustrating part. Behaviour backs it: an envelope of receipts per job, totalled
by hand; emailed supplier invoices printed and filed; everything handed to his accountant.
He reacted well to photographing receipts against a job, but that came after the pitch and
is not commitment. Recorded as new hypothesis **H9** in
[hypothesis table](./hypotheses.md).

**Documents seen (2026-11-17).** Two real examples, read locally and kept out of the repo
because they hold a customer's address and bank details:

- *His own bill to a private customer* (handwritten in a notebook, headed "Invoice"). It has a
  customer name and address, one lump sum for the work, deductions for an electrician and a
  tiler, one added part, a total and his bank details. It has **no date, no invoice number,
  no labour/materials split and no VAT line**. In the interview he said his invoices break
  down labour and materials; this one does not. One example, but behaviour beats description.
- *An emailed supplier invoice* (electrical wholesaler, PDF). It is fully itemised, with
  product codes, quantities, unit prices, VAT at 20% and a total, and is marked paid. There is
  **no job reference**, so matching it to a customer job is done by memory.

For H9 this means the supplier side already arrives as structured data, while the link to a
job and the trader's own bill are where the manual work sits. Both still need a timed
walkthrough (#141) before they count as evidence of pain.

**Follow-ups.** Walk through one real completed job envelope with him (#141); ask the G0b
auto-text question as past behaviour (#142).

## Anti-patterns

- ⚠️ **Averaging away the signal.** Six lukewarm "yeah I suppose" answers do not add up to one
  strong pain. Count specific past events only.
- ⚠️ **Letting Dad's enthusiasm carry the gate.** He is one data point out of six, and the most
  biased one.
- ⚠️ **Recording what you hoped to hear.** Fill the sheet within an hour, before memory edits it.
- ⚠️ **Treating "sounds good" as commitment** (Fitzpatrick). Only time, reputation or cash count.

## Open questions

- If missed calls are *not* top-3, what *is* the top pain across the six? That is the pivot candidate.
- Do the trades differ enough that a single wedge cannot serve them all?
